## Kubernetes rules on effective security context, coverage and RBAC (WP-3-6)

### Fixed

- **A hardened workload is clean.**
  A Deployment, StatefulSet, DaemonSet, ReplicaSet, Job, CronJob or Pod with a pod-level `seccompProfile: RuntimeDefault`, `runAsNonRoot`, dropped capabilities and limits no longer gets a hardening finding.
  The rules read the effective values the parser computes (a container value wins, else the pod value) instead of the raw container fields.
  `tests/fixtures/real_shapes/hardened/kubernetes/hardened_workloads.yaml` now scans with no Kubernetes rule finding.
- **`K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` was inverted.**
  It fired when a securityContext existed, so it flagged hardened workloads and missed bare ones.
  It now fires when a container has no seccomp profile or uses `Unconfined`, and a pod-level `RuntimeDefault` or `Localhost` satisfies every container that does not override it.
- **`K8S_NO_NETWORK_POLICY` and `K8S_DEFAULT_ALLOW_EGRESS` fired on the best-practice default-deny policy.**
  They sit on the workloads now and read `ingress_policy_covered` and `egress_policy_covered`.
  A NetworkPolicy node never matches any rule, a workload that a default-deny policy selects is silent, and an uncovered workload gets a low, heuristic finding whose description says that only policies in the same input were considered.
- **`K8S_AUTOMOUNT_DEFAULT` fired on every ServiceAccount.**
  It now fires when `automountServiceAccountToken` is not explicitly false on the ServiceAccount.
  `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_SA_TOKEN` reads the effective value (pod setting, else ServiceAccount setting, else true), so a ServiceAccount with `automountServiceAccountToken: false` silences both rules and a pod that sets it to true still fires over an account that sets it to false.
- **`K8S_NO_PSA_LABELS` fired on every namespace.**
  It reads the one key `pod-security.kubernetes.io/enforce`, so a labelled namespace is silent and an unlabelled one gets exactly one finding.
- **`K8S_NO_RESOURCE_LIMITS` needs both a CPU and a memory limit,** and `K8S_EPHEMERAL_STORAGE_UNBOUNDED` reads `has_ephemeral_storage_limit` instead of repeating the resource-limit check.
- **`K8S_INGRESS_NO_TLS` is judged on Ingresses that route traffic** and is heuristic, because a controller can terminate TLS from an annotation or at a cloud load balancer.
- **Role and RoleBinding rules were missing.**
  The wildcard rules covered only a ClusterRole and the admin rule only a ClusterRoleBinding.
  A role rule lists its role types and never matches a binding.
- **`K8S_HOST_PID` maps to `T1611` (Escape to Host)** instead of Container and Resource Discovery.
- The workload rules no longer carry a hand-written `tactics:` list.
  The engine derives the tactics from the techniques, so the two cannot disagree.

### Added

- **`K8S_HOST_IPC`** for `hostIPC: true` (medium, definitive).
- **`K8S_SERVICE_EXPOSES_ADMIN_PORT`** for a LoadBalancer or NodePort Service that publishes SSH, Telnet, RDP or WinRM as its port or target port (high, heuristic, `T1133`).
  The ports are the ones of `threatcode.vocab.ADMIN_PORTS`, and a test keeps the two in step.
- **Namespaced RBAC rules.**
  `K8S_ROLE_WILDCARD_VERBS`, `K8S_ROLE_WILDCARD_RESOURCES` and `K8S_ROLE_WILDCARD_API_GROUPS` fire on a Role at medium, medium and low, below the ClusterRole rules.
  `K8S_WILDCARD_RBAC_API_GROUPS` completes the ClusterRole set at medium.
  The six wildcard rules share the root cause `rbac_wildcard`.
- **Dangerous grants, as separate rules for a Role and a ClusterRole.**
  `K8S_RBAC_ESCALATION_VERBS` (escalate, bind, impersonate; high), `K8S_RBAC_SECRETS_READ` (get, list or watch on Secrets; high, heuristic, because `resourceNames` is not considered) and `K8S_RBAC_POD_EXEC` (create on `pods/exec`; medium).
- **Bindings to broad subjects.**
  `K8S_ANONYMOUS_PRIVILEGED_BINDING` is critical for a ClusterRoleBinding of cluster-admin, admin or edit to `system:anonymous`, `system:unauthenticated` or `system:authenticated`.
  `K8S_ANONYMOUS_BINDING` is high for any other binding of a role to those subjects, and never reports the discovery roles `system:public-info-viewer`, `system:basic-user` and `system:discovery`.
- **`K8S_ROLE_BINDING_ADMIN`** for a RoleBinding to the cluster-admin or admin ClusterRole (medium, because the grant stops at the namespace).
- **A shared root cause for the stacked hardening findings.**
  `K8S_NO_SECURITY_CONTEXT`, `K8S_NO_SECURITY_CONTEXT_DEFAULTS`, `K8S_RUN_AS_ROOT`, `K8S_WRITABLE_ROOT_FS`, `K8S_ALLOW_PRIVILEGE_ESCALATION`, `K8S_CAPABILITIES_NOT_DROPPED` and `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` carry `root_cause: container_hardening`.
  The two automount rules share `sa_token_automount`.
- **Examples for every Kubernetes rule.**
  All 39 rules have a bad and a good manifest under `tests/test_rules/cases/`, and the four `uncovered/kubernetes__*.txt` lists are empty.
  The pending marker on `K8S_INGRESS_NO_TLS` (WP-2-4) is deleted.
- **A rule integrity test** that fails when a Kubernetes rule reads a property the parser does not emit for its resource types, which is how `K8S_NO_PDB`, `K8S_NO_RESOURCE_QUOTAS` and the first `K8S_INGRESS_NO_TLS` could never fire.

### Changed

- **Calibration.**
  Every rule declares a confidence, and the severities respect the caps: absence rules stop at medium, heuristic rules at high, and critical is definitive only.
  `K8S_RUN_AS_ROOT` high to medium (heuristic, because the image may switch user), `K8S_ALLOW_PRIVILEGE_ESCALATION` high to medium, `K8S_WRITABLE_ROOT_FS`, `K8S_CAPABILITIES_NOT_DROPPED`, `K8S_NO_SECURITY_CONTEXT`, `K8S_NO_SECURITY_CONTEXT_DEFAULTS`, `K8S_EPHEMERAL_STORAGE_UNBOUNDED`, `K8S_DEFAULT_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`, `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_DEFAULT` and `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_SA_TOKEN` medium to low.
  `K8S_CLUSTER_ADMIN_BINDING` critical to high: it is critical only where the subject is anonymous or authenticated, which `K8S_ANONYMOUS_PRIVILEGED_BINDING` now reports.
  It also covers a ClusterRoleBinding to the admin ClusterRole.
  The three binding rules split an input between them, so one binding is one finding.
- **ATT&CK.**
  `K8S_ALLOW_PRIVILEGE_ESCALATION` maps to `T1548`, `K8S_WRITABLE_ROOT_FS` to `T1105`, `K8S_DEFAULT_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` to `T1078.001`, the coverage rules to `T1046` and `T1048`, escalation verbs to `T1098.006`, Secrets read to `T1552.007` and pod exec to `T1609`.
  Every technique exists in the ATT&CK v19.2 data and none is revoked, which a test checks.
- **Descriptions.**
  `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_SA_TOKEN` and `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_DEFAULT` say which level each one judges, and `K8S_NO_RESOURCE_LIMITS` and `K8S_EPHEMERAL_STORAGE_UNBOUNDED` say which resources each one judges.

### Removed

- `K8S_NO_RESOURCE_QUOTAS` and `K8S_NO_PDB`.
  They asked whether a ResourceQuota or PodDisruptionBudget exists somewhere else, which one input cannot tell, so a single manifest got a finding on every namespace and every Deployment.
- `K8S_NO_LIVENESS_PROBE` and `K8S_NO_READINESS_PROBE`.
  They were reliability advice with no attacker in them and were mapped to Endpoint Denial of Service.
- All four are listed in `deprecated_ids`, so a suppression that names one warns that the rule no longer exists.

### Golden differences

Every difference is inside `tests/fixtures/golden/*/findings/kubernetes__*.json` (61 files), and `scripts/check_golden_scope.py` reports no change outside that glob.
`graph.json` and `model.json` do not move.
Four effects repeat across inputs, and the list below names only what is specific to an input.

- Probe findings disappear wherever a workload had them.
- The severities above change on every finding of the rules concerned.
- The coverage findings move from the NetworkPolicy to the workloads it does not cover, so an input with workloads and no policy gains a `kubernetes__networking.json` that names them.
- `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` appears on workloads that have no securityContext at all and disappears where a pod-level profile is set.

Per input:

- `kubernetes__secure_deployment.yml`: gains `K8S_NO_NETWORK_POLICY`, `K8S_DEFAULT_ALLOW_EGRESS` and `K8S_EPHEMERAL_STORAGE_UNBOUNDED`, all low, because the fixture has no policy and no ephemeral-storage limit.
  It keeps `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` (the fixture sets none) and `K8S_DEFAULT_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`, now low.
- `kubernetes__insecure_deployment.yml`, `kubernetes__multi_container.yml`, `kubernetes__mixed_documents.yml`, `real_shapes__kubernetes__kubectl_get_all.{yaml,json}` and `kubernetes__workload_kinds.yml`: no policy in the input, so each workload gains the two low coverage findings.
- `kubernetes__multi_resource.yml`: `K8S_CLUSTER_ADMIN_BINDING` critical to high.
  The ClusterRole with wildcards gains `K8S_WILDCARD_RBAC_API_GROUPS`, `K8S_RBAC_SECRETS_READ` and `K8S_RBAC_POD_EXEC`, which a wildcard grant does include.
  The ingress-only NetworkPolicy no longer fires; the Deployment it selects gains `K8S_DEFAULT_ALLOW_EGRESS`, and the workload gains `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE`.
- `kubernetes__ingress_to_cluster_admin.yml`: the same three RBAC findings on its wildcard ClusterRole.
- `kubernetes__network_policies.yml`: the policies stop firing.
  The workloads that no policy covers get the findings instead (one ingress and two egress), and the covered ones get none.
- `kubernetes__graph_stack.yml`: the two policy findings leave the NetworkPolicy and nothing replaces them, because it covers its workloads, and two workloads gain `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE`.
- `kubernetes__pod_level_context.yml`: `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` disappears, because the profile is set at the pod level.
- `real_shapes__kubernetes__shop_stack.yaml`: the largest false-positive removal.
  `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_DEFAULT`, `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_SA_TOKEN`, `K8S_NO_NETWORK_POLICY`, two `K8S_DEFAULT_ALLOW_EGRESS` and `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` disappear, since the ServiceAccount disables the mount, the policies cover the workloads and the pod sets a profile.
  `K8S_EPHEMERAL_STORAGE_UNBOUNDED` appears.
- `real_shapes__kubernetes__workloads.yaml`: the two `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` findings on workloads with a pod-level profile disappear, one appears on a workload with none, and every workload gains the two coverage findings.
- `real_shapes__helm__platform__Chart.yaml`: `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_DEFAULT` and `K8S_AUTOMOUNT_SA_TOKEN` disappear because the chart's ServiceAccount disables the mount, `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` leaves the platform Deployment (it sets a pod-level profile) and lands on the redis StatefulSet (it sets none), the secrets Role gains `K8S_RBAC_SECRETS_READ`, and the coverage and ephemeral-storage findings follow the rules above.
- `helm__*`, `real_shapes__helm__scaffold__Chart.yaml`, `kubernetes__deployment_list.json`, `kubernetes__list_pod_deployment.yaml`: the repeated effects only, plus `K8S_NO_SECCOMP_PROFILE` on the workloads without a securityContext.

### Carryover handled

- From WP-2-4: the rules now read `has_seccomp_profile`, `effective_automount_sa_token`, the ServiceAccount's `automount_sa_token`, `ingress_policy_covered`, `egress_policy_covered` and `has_ephemeral_storage_limit`.
- From WP-2-4: the pending case file of `K8S_INGRESS_NO_TLS` is deleted.
- From WP-1-1: the Role and RoleBinding rule duplicates, and the `has_pdb` property (the rules that read it are removed).
- From WP-1-5: the Kubernetes slice of the ATT&CK retag, with tactics derived from techniques and a test that every technique exists and is not revoked.
- WI-R4 and RQ-21 slices for Kubernetes: no rule reads a property that nothing emits, and the stacked hardening rules share a root cause.
